\title{A Bayesian seamless phase II/III dose-optimisation design with a weighted composite selection endpoint and co-primary confirmatory endpoints}

\author[1,2]{Zerong Chen}

\authormark{CHEN}
\titlemark{Seamless design with composite selection and co-primary confirmation}

\address[1]{\orgdiv{National Heart and Lung Institute}, \orgname{Imperial College London}, \orgaddress{\state{London}, \country{United Kingdom}}}
\address[2]{\orgdiv{Imperial Clinical Trials Unit}, \orgname{Imperial College London}, \orgaddress{\state{London}, \country{United Kingdom}}}

\corres{Zerong Chen, National Heart and Lung Institute, Imperial College London, London, United Kingdom. \email{z.chen@imperial.ac.uk}}

\abstract[Abstract]{Confirmatory trials with $K$ binary co-primary endpoints require every endpoint to succeed, so the type II error inflates with $K$ and the sample size rises accordingly. In a seamless phase II/III dose-optimisation design this cost is paid once per candidate dose at the selection stage. We propose a design in which the $M$ doses are compared at the interim on a single pre-specified weighted composite of the binary components, and the selected dose is confirmed on the full co-primary criterion with the stage-1 data included. Both functionals are projections of the same Dirichlet--multinomial cell-probability vector, so the inter-endpoint dependence is determined by the model rather than assumed. We show that the co-primary false-claim rate is controlled at level $\alpha$ for any selection rule, futility rule and stage-2 sample size, by closed testing over doses within an inverse-normal combination and an intersection--union decision over endpoints; the composite therefore affects power but not the type I error. Under parallel dose effects a second-moment argument brackets the ratio of stage-1 sample sizes needed for a given selection accuracy below one, and we characterise the configurations in which the composite gate fails: discordance between the composite and co-primary optima, and failure on a single component, which a composite registers weakly. In simulation calibrated to a four-serogroup meningococcal vaccine example, the design reaches the same claim probability as a co-primary-gated seamless design with 7--15\% fewer expected subjects when the optimal dose is best on most endpoints, and is inferior when the optimum is a trade-off between endpoints. A design rule based on the power-versus-expected-sample-size frontier is given.}

\keywords{adaptive seamless design; co-primary endpoints; composite endpoint; Dirichlet--multinomial; dose optimisation; treatment selection}
