\title{A Bayesian seamless phase II/III dose-selection design with a weighted composite selection endpoint and co-primary confirmatory endpoints}

\author[1,2]{Zerong Chen}

\authormark{CHEN}
\titlemark{Seamless design with composite selection and co-primary confirmation}

\address[1]{\orgdiv{National Heart and Lung Institute}, \orgname{Imperial College London}, \orgaddress{\state{London}, \country{United Kingdom}}}
\address[2]{\orgdiv{Imperial Clinical Trials Unit}, \orgname{Imperial College London}, \orgaddress{\state{London}, \country{United Kingdom}}}

\corres{Zerong Chen, National Heart and Lung Institute, Imperial College London, London, United Kingdom. \email{z.chen@imperial.ac.uk}}

\abstract[Abstract]{Confirmatory trials with $K$ binary co-primary endpoints succeed only if every endpoint succeeds, which raises the sample size. In a seamless phase II/III dose-selection design this cost is also paid at the interim, once for each of the $M$ candidate doses. We propose a design that selects the dose at the interim on a pre-specified weighted composite of the endpoints and confirms the selected dose on the co-primary criterion with the data of both stages. A Dirichlet--multinomial model for the $2^K$ response patterns gives the posterior of both criteria. The final analysis is a closed test over all $MK$ dose--endpoint hypotheses with inverse-normal combination of stage-wise $p$-values. We prove that the probability of a false co-primary claim is at most $\alpha$ for any selection rule, futility rule and stage-2 sample size. Closing each endpoint separately over doses does not control this error, and its false-claim rate reached $0.055$ at $\alpha=0.025$ in simulation. In simulations based on a four-serogroup meningococcal vaccine trial, the design reached a power of $0.80$--$0.90$ with about $10$\% fewer expected subjects than a seamless design that applies the co-primary criterion at both stages when dose effects were parallel across endpoints ($9$--$12$\% with uniform weights, $5$--$9$\% with calibrated weights). The saving was at most $4$\% when one endpoint separated the doses, and the composite selected worse when the best dose was a trade-off between endpoints. A per-endpoint futility check stops the composite design when one endpoint shows no effect, at a cost of one to three points of the saving. We give a rule for choosing between the two selection criteria.}

\keywords{adaptive seamless design, closed testing, co-primary endpoints, composite endpoint, Dirichlet--multinomial, dose selection, treatment selection}
